# India in Tax.cal

Rule versions `IN-2026-27-v1` (Tax Year 2026-27, Income-tax Act, 2025) and `IN-2025-26-legacy-v1` (FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27, Income-tax Act, 1961). Both have the same amounts, because Budget 2026 changed no personal rates. The law, the section numbers and the terminology differ. The tool output is authoritative. Use this page to explain it, not to calculate.

## Terminology

- The **Income-tax Act, 2025** applies from 1 April 2026. It uses one **tax year** (1 April – 31 March) in place of "previous year" and "assessment year".
- **Tax Year 2026-27** is income earned 1 April 2026 – 31 March 2027.
- **AY 2026-27** is income earned in **FY 2025-26** under the Income-tax Act, 1961, the return most people file in 2026. It is a different obligation from Tax Year 2026-27, even though "2026-27" appears in both.

## New regime (section 202; the default)

- Standard deduction ₹75,000 from salary.
- Slabs on taxable income: up to ₹4 lakh nil; ₹4–8 lakh 5%; ₹8–12 lakh 10%; ₹12–16 lakh 15%; ₹16–20 lakh 20%; ₹20–24 lakh 25%; above ₹24 lakh 30%.
- Rebate (section 156, residents only): up to ₹60,000 when taxable income is at most ₹12 lakh, so a salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax. Just above ₹12 lakh of taxable income, marginal relief caps the tax at the income above ₹12 lakh.
- Most deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest on a self-occupied home) are not available.

## Old regime (opt-in)

- Standard deduction ₹50,000.
- Basic exemption for residents: ₹2.5 lakh below 60, ₹3 lakh at 60–79, ₹5 lakh at 80+. Non-residents always get ₹2.5 lakh. Rates are 5%, 20% and 30%.
- Rebate up to ₹12,500 when taxable income is at most ₹5 lakh (residents only).
- Deductions the user supplies, capped by the tool: 80C ₹1.5 lakh, 80CCD(1B) ₹50,000, 80D up to ₹1 lakh, self-occupied home-loan interest ₹2 lakh. The HRA exemption and "other" are taken as given.

## Both regimes

- Surcharge on income tax: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime only (the new regime caps at 25%). Marginal relief applies at each threshold.
- Health and Education Cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge.

## What to ask, and what to state as a default

- Regime: default new. State it, and offer `compare_tax_regimes`.
- Residency: default resident. NRIs get no rebate, so ask if the user mentions living abroad.
- Age band: only changes the old regime.
- Deductions: only the amounts the user gives. Never assume 80C is "maxed out".

## Not covered

Business or professional income, capital gains (and how they interact with the rebate), HUFs, firms and companies, foreign income or assets, TDS, GST filing and ITR preparation. Point to the Income Tax Department (incometaxindia.gov.in) or a chartered accountant.

## GST and fuel (indirect estimate)

Category estimates under the two-slab GST in force since 22 September 2025: most staples nil and much packaged food 5%; restaurants 5% without input tax credit; many goods and services 18%. Petrol is outside GST: central excise plus Delhi VAT is about 28% of the pump price. Confidence is low or medium by category. Present these as estimates.
