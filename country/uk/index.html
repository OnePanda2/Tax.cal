How much tax do you really pay in the United Kingdom?

On a £40,000 salary in the United Kingdom, about £7,680 is taken directly as income tax and national insurance — roughly 19%. But once you add the VAT and fuel duty hidden in everyday spending (about £1,552 a year), your real effective tax rate is around 23%.

Questions

United Kingdom tax, answered

How much tax do you really pay in the United Kingdom?

On £40,000 a year, an estimated 23% of your income becomes tax in the United Kingdom once you count £1,552 of VAT and fuel duty on top of the £7,680 taken directly. Your exact figure depends on your deductions and spending.

What is the difference between my payslip tax rate and my real tax rate?

Your payslip shows the direct rate — about 19% here (income tax plus national insurance). Your real rate also includes the VAT and fuel duty you pay whenever you spend, which most people never add up. On this salary that is roughly £1,552 more a year.

Pay your pension by salary sacrifice — does it help in the United Kingdom?

Contributions come out before both Income Tax and National Insurance — a rare double saving most employers offer.
